OUR APPROACH
Education is a
social issue.
The story of a child's learning starts outside the classroom. It starts in the household, the block, the bus route. Here's the problem we're responding to, the theory behind our response, and the proof that it's working.
01The Problemwhy we exist
In Charlottesville, the literacy gap between Black and White students is 49 percentage points. It's the highest in Virginia. — VDOE Standards of Learning, 2024
Chronic absenteeism. Household instability. Our kids don't have a "school problem." They have a life problem that school is trying to solve alone.

02Theory of Changethe argument
You can't fix a child's outcomes without changing the household around them.
Three assumptions. Take any one away and the model collapses.
ASSUMPTION 1
Proximity matters
Services inside the school the child already attends. Not a referral across town.
ASSUMPTION 2
Parents first
A parent who's thriving is the single biggest predictor of a child who's thriving.
ASSUMPTION 3
Relationship, not triage
A coach who knows the family's name, and has been there for two years, can do what a case manager can't.
03Dual-Generation Modelwho we reach
Most programs pick a lane: reach the kid, or reach the parent. We do both, in the same household, at the same time.
CHILD-SIDE
What we do for the kid
  • Embedded Pathway Coach at their school
  • On-site tutoring during the school day
  • Walking Bus to / from class
  • Food pantry access when the household needs it
PARENT-SIDE
What we do for the parent
  • Parent Time, the weekly cohort
  • P.A.C.T. Time, structured parent-child engagement
  • Up to $7K micro-financing, matched to family goals
  • School navigation & advocacy

OUR APPROACH — AN ARGUMENT IN FIVE PARTS
Five questions nobody else is answering about how education works in Charlottesville.
Each panel poses the question we heard from a family, a teacher, or a funder — and what we've learned to say in return.
01
THE PROBLEM
"Why is Charlottesville's achievement gap the worst in the state?"
A wealthy college town. $18.5K per pupil. Seven school sites inside three miles. And a 49-point literacy gap.
WHAT WE'VE LEARNED
Education is a social issue.
49pp
Reading-SOL gap, highest in VA. The schools are not the problem. The conditions around the schools are.
→ Supporting data: absenteeism, household instability, generational poverty
02
THEORY OF CHANGE
"If you can't reform the schools, what can you change?"
We don't run schools. We don't want to. We change the three conditions that determine whether a child shows up ready to learn.
WHAT WE'VE LEARNED
Proximity · Parents · Relationship.
Proximity — services inside the building, not across town.
Parents first — a thriving adult is the strongest predictor of a thriving child.
Relationship, not triage — a coach who sticks for two years.
03
DUAL-GENERATION MODEL
"Do you work with the kid, or with the parent?"
Yes.
Every dollar that goes into a child flows through the household. We fund both sides of that equation, from the same coach, in the same week.
WHAT WE'VE LEARNED
Both — same household, same week.
CHILD-SIDE
In-school
Coach · tutoring · pantry · Walking Bus
PARENT-SIDE
In-cohort
Parent Time · PACT · $7K · advocacy
04
CONSTELLATION OF CARE
"So — are these three programs, or one?"
On paper: three. In the life of a single Charlottesville family: one woven network of the same trusted people.
WHAT WE'VE LEARNED
